# Harrington POC learning spine

The proof of concept keeps Marble's complete prerequisite graph in memory but
shows the family a deliberately small learning surface. This is a **focus
layer**, not a reduced copy of the curriculum: prerequisites outside the focus
layer remain available when Harrington explains why a topic is or is not ready.

## Daily shape

On a typical home day Harrington should offer:

1. a short literacy choice;
2. a short numeracy choice;
3. two or three interest-led invitations drawn from current interests, the
   season, an active project, or a real family need; and
4. an easy way to record what actually happened, including unplanned learning.

The children can choose the activity and order. The parent view quietly checks
that essential skills continue to move and that no area has disappeared for too
long. A choice is not labelled as curriculum coverage unless the recorded
evidence supports that claim.

## POC focus areas

These names match domains in the current Marble taxonomy.

### Literacy — configurable hard gates

- Phonics & Word Reading
- Handwriting & Transcription
- Reading Comprehension
- Writing Composition
- Speaking & Listening

The exact gates are age- and child-appropriate. Evidence may be spoken,
written, performed, observed, or captured inside a project; a digital quiz is
not required when another form demonstrates the skill more faithfully.

### Numeracy — configurable hard gates

- Counting & Cardinality
- Number Representation & Place Value
- Addition & Subtraction
- Mathematical Thinking

Harrington should prefer practical evidence—measuring timber, sharing food,
keeping score, budgeting, building, or explaining a strategy—while retaining
the option of a short direct check when the evidence is ambiguous.

### Discovery — observational and portfolio evidence

- Scientific Inquiry
- Ecosystems & Habitats
- Insects & Minibeasts
- Organisms & Life Processes
- Weather & Climate
- Earth's Systems

Only the inquiry process belongs in the stable spine. The content lane should
move with a child's interest and what is observable on the property that day.

### Projects and life practice — evidence can cross domains

- Speaking & Listening
- Friendship & Cooperation
- Responsible Decision-Making
- Money & Finance
- Measurement
- Matter & Materials
- Forces & Motion

A project can contribute evidence to several domains, but it must record the
specific behaviour or artefact that supports each claim. Useful outcomes
include a construction, drawing, performance, presentation, meal, market plan,
experiment, negotiated agreement, or service to someone else.

## Child treatment in the POC

- **Early learner:** invitations, play, stories, singing, movement, imitation,
  and parent observations only. No visible targets or mastery gates.
- **Six-year-old hands-on learner:** building, movement, sport, nature, and
  practical challenges are preferred ways into literacy and numeracy.
- **Nine-year-old artistic learner:** art, music, stories, and performance are
  preferred entry points, with a small amount of adult-curated choice to help
  start and sustain work.

Use synthetic profiles with these characteristics during development. Real
names and records should wait for the storage, privacy, and export review.

## Not part of this first spine

- a full-year syllabus;
- school-term scheduling;
- strict Steiner block replication;
- generated First Nations cultural content;
- scores shown to children;
- mastery gates for creativity, curiosity, motivation, or wellbeing; and
- a claim that every interest-led activity automatically covers curriculum.

## First product test

For one synthetic child, the parent should be able to select an interest such
as building a bird shelter and receive:

- three meaningfully different activity invitations;
- the relevant ready-now skills from the focus areas above;
- any hard prerequisite that genuinely blocks a literacy or numeracy claim;
- suggested observable evidence for the project; and
- a plain-language record after the activity, with scores visible only in the
  parent view.

If this interaction is useful, the next iteration can add the seasonal rhythm,
project planning, and family-specific curriculum mappings without first having
to rebuild the inherited platform.
